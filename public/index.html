How percentages work

"Percent" means "out of 100". 15% is 15 out of every 100, or 0.15 as a decimal. Every calculation above comes from that one idea: turn the percentage into a decimal by dividing by 100, then multiply or divide.

Finding a percentage of a number

Divide the percentage by 100, then multiply by the number. Use this for tips, tax, commission or a share of a bill. Open this calculator.

15% of 240 = 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = 36

What percent one number is of another

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. Use this for test scores, survey results or how much of a budget is spent. Open this calculator.

36 out of 240 = 36 ÷ 240 × 100 = 15%

Percent increase and decrease

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. A positive answer is an increase; a negative one is a decrease. Open this calculator.

From 80 to 100 = (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = +25%

Adding or removing a percentage

To add 15%, multiply by 1.15. To remove 15%, multiply by 0.85. This is how sales tax, price rises and pay rises are worked out. Open this calculator.

240 increased by 15% = 240 × 1.15 = 276

Working out a discount

Multiply the price by the discount to get the saving, then take that away from the price. Open this calculator.

59.99 with 20% off: saving 59.99 × 0.20 = 12.00, so you pay 47.99

Finding the original number

If you know a part and what percentage it is, divide the part by the percentage as a decimal. This undoes a "percent of" calculation — handy for working back to a pre-tax price. Open this calculator.

36 is 15% of 36 ÷ 0.15 = 240

Common questions

Is a 50% increase undone by a 50% decrease?

No. 100 increased by 50% is 150, and 150 decreased by 50% is 75. The decrease is taken from the bigger number, so you end up lower than you started. To undo a 50% increase you need a 33.33% decrease.

What's the difference between percent and percentage points?

Percentage points are the plain gap between two percentages. An interest rate going from 4% to 5% rose by 1 percentage point — but that's a 25% increase in the rate itself.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. 300 is 150% of 200, and a price that goes from 20 to 50 has risen by 150%. A decrease, though, can't be more than 100% unless the value goes below zero.

How do I remove tax from a price that already includes it?

Divide by 1 plus the tax rate. A price of 120 including 20% tax was 120 ÷ 1.20 = 100 before tax. Use "Find the original" with 120 and 120%.

Why does the answer show up to four decimal places?

So you can round it the way you need. Money is usually rounded to two decimal places, which the discount calculator does for you.
